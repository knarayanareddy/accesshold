# AccessHold — Product Truth

## Audience

Disabled travelers and companions managing existing bookings, plus specialist travel advocates handling disruptions. Users may be tired, using assistive technology, or reviewing time-sensitive information in a noisy airport. The interface must reduce re-explanation and verification work rather than celebrate automation.

## Core job

**Understand what changed, what remains evidenced, and what can be done next—without weakening a hard access constraint.**

## Operating context

- Responsive web for detailed review and a constrained Alexa rich card for in-context decisions.
- Intermittent connectivity, time pressure, screen readers, zoom, and shared-device privacy.
- Sensitive access needs and provider documents; no advertising or playful data visualization.

## Product personality

Calm, exact, discreet, and advocative. It should feel like a well-prepared travel advocate's evidence folder, not an airport departure board, chatbot, or generic travel marketplace.

## Voice

- `Aircraft changed. One hard requirement needs review.`
- `Provider confirmed · 20 Oct, 09:30 UTC`
- `Two options preserve all currently verified requirements. One detail remains unknown.`
- `Request sent. Waiting for the property's confirmation.`

Avoid: `Great news!`, `AI verified`, `Accessible guaranteed`, `Don't worry`, or legal-sounding certainty.

## Durable constraints

- Evidence and uncertainty remain visible at the point of decision.
- Approval is explicit, plan-bound, and readable without voice.
- Unknown never appears as satisfied.
- Provider request acceptance never appears as a completed booking change.
- Rich cards and web views share the same underlying status language.

## Visual anti-references

- Purple-gradient AI assistant landing pages.
- Claymorphism, rounded toy-like cards, confetti, or celebratory disruption animations.
- Dense flight-comparison tables dominated by prices rather than constraint consequences.
- Traffic-light-only accessibility indicators and hidden evidence drawers.

## Design success signals

- A traveler can identify the changed asset and unresolved requirement within ten seconds.
- A reviewer can distinguish documented, provider-confirmed, stale, and unknown evidence without color.
- No user mistakes a sent request or sandbox receipt for a confirmed live itinerary.
- Keyboard and screen-reader users can compare options and approve or hand off without assistance.
